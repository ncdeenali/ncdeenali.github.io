Case NV · Steganalysis

2nd place · National Technology Parade

NinjaVision

Is there a secret hiding in this photo?

The question
Can you tell if a JPEG has a hidden message, even after WhatsApp, social media or a screenshot has chewed it up? Most detectors fall apart once images come from a different source than they trained on.
What I did
Led the team and designed a small Siamese MLP (297,473 parameters) that never judges an image on its own. It compares the image's 257 bin DCT histogram against reference covers routed from the same kind of source. We trained it in three stages, from lab benchmarks to messy real world photos, and added gradient heatmaps so an investigator can see why an image was flagged.
What came out of it
88.1% AUC on NINJA, our dataset of real JPEGs from cameras, WhatsApp, social media, screenshots and web downloads, now on Mendeley Data. The paper is being submitted to IEEE Access.
  • PyTorch
  • jpegio
  • NumPy / SciPy
  • scikit-learn

Case AL · Arabic NLP

3rd place · Altibbi NLP Challenge

Arabic Medical NLP

Which doctor should this patient see?

The question
Route 82k real patient questions to the right specialty, written in Gulf, Egyptian and Levantine dialects, sometimes in Latin letters, sometimes mixed with English.

Case LA · Applied Analytical Models

Full stack ML portal

Smart Laptop Upgrade Advisor

What should you upgrade, how much will it cost, and can you trust the answer?

The question
Given someone's laptop and what they use it for, recommend the right upgrade and its cost, and explain the decision in words a normal person understands.
What I did
Built a Flask portal that combines five pieces: an XGBoost cost model and a Random Forest upgrade classifier explained with SHAP and LIME, then rewritten into plain English by a local Mistral model. A version with fairness mitigation you can compare side by side. A fuzzy logic suitability checker. An Isolation Forest that flags laptop specs that don't add up. And a Q-learning chatbot that learns which questions to ask before it recommends anything.
What came out of it
Cost model R² 0.94 (up from 0.24 with linear regression), upgrade classifier 98.5% accuracy, anomaly detector 0.775 AUC on injected anomalies.

Research

AI for Justice & Forensics

Fake evidence, hidden messages, manipulated media, and data that lies on purpose. I want to build AI that catches them and can explain why.

The thread

Every project I've loved ends up being about the same question: what is this data not telling me? Sometimes it's a message hidden in a photo. Sometimes it's a mislabeled dataset. Sometimes a sensor with perfectly valid credentials is lying.

I like the mathy side of it most. Signal statistics, graphs, the models that only work if you understand why they work. I also care that the answer comes with an explanation, because a forensic tool nobody can question shouldn't be trusted.

Capstone, then paper

Feb 2025 · Now

NinjaVision: cross domain JPEG steganalysis

Steganalysis detects whether an image carries a hidden message. Lab detectors usually break on real world photos because every camera, app and compression step changes the image statistics (cover source mismatch). NinjaVision attacks that with a small, statistical model that never judges an image alone.

  • A Siamese MLP with 297,473 parameters on 257 bin DCT histograms, a small fraction of the size of modern deep steganalyzers.
  • Before deciding, it routes each image to reference covers from the same kind of source, so a WhatsApp photo is judged against WhatsApp photos.
  • A three stage curriculum: lab benchmarks (BOSSBase, BOWS2), then ALASKA2, then real world photos, against three modern embedding schemes (J‑UNIWARD, JMiPOD, UERD).
  • 88.1% AUC on NINJA, our dataset of real JPEGs from five sources: cameras, WhatsApp, social media (Instagram, Pinterest, X), screenshots and web downloads.
  • Gradient based heatmaps that show where the suspect and reference histograms differ the most, so an investigator can check the call.
Paper N. Ali, R. Al-Khanji, Z. Al Shaikh Ali, F. A. Khasawneh, E. Taqieddin. NinjaVision: A Statistical Siamese Approach for Cross Domain JPEG Steganalysis Using DCT Histogram Features. Being submitted to IEEE Access, 2026.

Research Assistant

Aug 2026 · Now

Hashemite University

Cyber resilient AI for EV charging

Electric vehicle charging networks run on data from cars, chargers and the grid. If any of it is wrong, or lies on purpose, the whole system makes bad decisions. I'm helping design the next stage of the group's work: a charging ecosystem that sees trouble coming and keeps working under attack. It focuses on three gaps.

  • Herding. When every driver gets the same advice, they all rush to the same chargers, and the crowd only shows up after the fact.
  • Authenticated liars. Sources with valid credentials that still send false data.
  • Battery wear. Charging decisions rarely count the cost of wearing down a car's battery.

This work is ongoing, so I'm keeping the details offline for now.
